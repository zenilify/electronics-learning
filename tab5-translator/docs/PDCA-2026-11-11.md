## 2 · Measurements and findings

### 2.0 Tools that made the night possible

- **Console without reset.** `tools/console.py` (pyserial) reboots the Tab5 on every use. `tools/console_nr.py` opens the port with
  plain `os.open` + termios, never writes DTR/RTS and clears HUPCL. Measured: log timestamps keep counting across sessions
  (3 097 s → 3 109 s → 3 113 s). This is what makes a 24 h `zfix` watch possible at all.
- **`flash.sh backup` was not a backup.** It keeps segments in one fixed folder and skips those already there; the 7 Nov run
  stopped at segment 1 with 14 older segments in place. `tools/backup_full.sh` reads into a fresh folder per run.
- **Screenshots.** `python3 tools/screenshot.py out.jpg --pre "key a"` — any screen, no reset, about 3 s. Also good for the
  M5Stack deck (clean captures instead of photos of the panel).
- Console additions: `usb on|off|host`, `usb`, `io`, `ext5v on|off`, `chg on|off|once`, `bcam <ip>|auto`, `shot [q]`,
  `uiwd reset`, `t5b R …`. `status` now ends with `v= up= usb-a=[…]`, plus `status2` (camera, dropped frames, UI restarts)
  and `status3` (charger kicks).

### 2.1 USB-A power (cycle 1)

The Espressif BSP only raises `USB5V_EN` (expander 0x44 pin 3) inside `bsp_usb_host_start()`; PAPP never called it. After the
driver's chip reset every expander pin is "output, high-Z, pull-down", so the switch input sits low. M5Stack's own firmware
raises it at boot. The schematic (U27 MT9700, about 1.3 A limit) and the three official init sequences are in the research
notes (§3.3). Read-back after the fix: `0x44 dir=ff out=09 hiz=f6`; with the charger on: `out=89 hiz=76`.

### 2.2 Why the powered camera was still "not found" (cycle 4)

The XIAO joins the strongest SSID it knows, decided again at every power-up. On the bench both the home Wi-Fi and the travel
router `kaizen-tr` are in range and its choice **changed from boot to boot** (seen: kaizen-tr, home, kaizen-tr, home). The
Tab5 was pinned to the home Wi-Fi for the remotes bench. mDNS does not cross the travel router.

Fix without touching the camera: its console is its USB port, so its boot log (`xcam: joining wifi2 "kaizen-tr"`,
`xcam: ip 192.168.29.246`) arrives on the cable that powers it. The Tab5 now cycles the port at boot (so the camera always
reboots and says where it is), listens, checks `/health`, and if the address cannot be reached while the picker is on Auto it
joins the camera's network once. mDNS still works and wins when it answers.

State left: picker on **Auto** (was: pinned to wifi0), Tab5 on `kaizen-tr`. Revert with console `wifi 0`.

### 2.3 The camera freeze (cycle 5) — not solved, contained

- Signature (decoded from the watchdog dump): LVGL task spinning in `lv_refr.c:1500 wait_for_flushing`, i.e. the display flush
  never reported "done". Same as landmine 326 (8 Nov). Seen once tonight in roughly 60 camera sessions.
- Tested and **not** the cause: 15 truncated + 15 corrupted JPEG frames in the hardware decoder with the preview running.
  The decoder times out (300 ms each) or reports a data-unit error, and the UI carries on.
- Not tested: `CONFIG_BSP_LCD_USE_DMA2D=n` (takes the 2D-DMA engine out of the display path). Without a way to trigger the
  freeze there is nothing to compare, so I left the display path alone (§4 D-4).
- Containment: `main.c` watches the 100 ms UI timer; 15 s of silence → log, count in NVS `uistall`, restart. Verified
  at 02:29 by holding the display lock on purpose: restart at 15 s, both remotes linked again 13 s later, camera found
  again. The counter was reset afterwards (`ui_restarts=0`); if it is not 0 one morning, the freeze happened.
  **That verification did not hold for the later builds**: at 03:48 the same kind of restart ended in a boot loop
  (§2.10). Since build c17 the watchdog's restart is a reset of the whole chip. Tested on c17 through the same call
  (`reboot`); the forced-stall test itself was not repeated on c17, because its test hook is not in normal builds.
- Final soak on 0.4.2 (20 open / capture / close cycles, 7.5 min): 20/20 captures HTTP 200 (average 409 ms, slowest
  668 ms), 0 watchdog hits, 0 UI restarts, 0 decoder errors, 0 dropped frames (`~/t5/exp-final-soak.log`).
- Watch item: free internal RAM went from 298 kB to 187 kB after opening eight apps in one boot (each app builds its
  screen once and keeps it). Measured per app in cycle 16: §2.9.
- Side observation, explained: `ocr vl HTTP -1 after 0 ms -> engine=ocr` (4 of 48 captures) is the designed fallback —
  the better OCR engine did not answer within its 8 s limit and the fast one took over. Only the "0 ms" was wrong (the
  time was not recorded on a timeout); fixed.

### 2.4 Battery charging (cycle 6) — enabled, start-up unreliable on a PC port

| Situation (USB power from cachy) | Battery |
|---|---|
| charger pin low — every PAPP build before tonight | 0.00 A, 8.14 V: **not charged at all** |
| pin raised at second 20 of a boot | +0.31…0.32 A, voltage climbing to 8.23 V |
| same boot, a few minutes later (camera tests, Wi-Fi switches in between) | 0.00 A with the pin still high |
| pin raised at second 3 of the boot | charged on one boot, did not on two |
| pin low 8 s then high | charging again |
| supervisor kick, pin low 0.3 s | did not restart it (2 of 2) |
| supervisor builds, pin raised at second 18 | charging on 3 of 8 boots; on one of them it charged through the whole 7.5-minute camera soak (8.32 V, 0.31 A) |

The firmware's current sign was inverted (charging read negative, so the bolt icon could never show); fixed.
One explanation, **not measured**: a PC USB port (0.9 A) and the Tab5's 1 A input diode cannot always feed system + camera
+ charger at once, and the charger gives up when the input sags. It does not explain everything (it charged through a
camera soak on one boot). Check with the wall charger (§4 D-3). The supervisor now
gives the pin a 3 s low pulse when the pack sits idle below 8.25 V on external power, at most every 3 minutes.

### 2.5 ESP-NOW (cycle 7)

- Chain tested: StickS3 (firmware `0.2.0-espnow`) → ESP-NOW, channel 1 → StampS3 running `espnow-bridge` 0.1.0 → USB →
  `tools/bridge_relay.py` on cachy (stands in for the cable) → Tab5 console `t5b R …` → `remotes.c` → ack back the same way.
- The remote finds the bridge by pinging on channels 1–13 (one sweep, about 0.4 s) and never joins an access point.
- Same datagrams as over UDP: HMAC, allow-list, nonce and acks stay end-to-end; the bridge only forwards bytes.
- Numbers: 80 events, 80 acks, 0 lost, 80 on the first try; ack round trip 21.0 / 24.0 / 28.0 / 29.0 ms (min / median /
  p95 / max). The relay measured the Tab5's share at 13.0 / 15.8 / 18.9 ms. UDP on the same bench was about 3–5 ms.
- Not built: the physical link bridge → Tab5 (UART on the Grove port, or USB). `EXT5V_EN` (Grove 5 V) is off by default;
  console `ext5v on` switches it.
- M5Dial, same chain (03:19): 80 events, 80 acks, 0 lost, 79 on the first try; 22.0 / 25.0 / 30.0 / 82.0 ms.
- The proven remotes firmware (0.1.2) is back on the StickS3 and the Dial (both linked over Wi-Fi again); the StampS3
  posts to `/line/event` again (`-> 200`).

### 2.6 Who receives the PAPP bearer token (found while working on the camera — not fixed for the "brain")

The Tab5 puts the PAPP bearer into every request to whatever it takes for the back camera and for the MacBook offline
brain, and both were found by mDNS (`_pappcam._tcp`, `_papp._tcp`). Any device on the same Wi-Fi can announce those names
and would be sent the token (and, for the brain, the audio). On a hotel or factory network that is a real exposure.
- Camera: the address told over the USB cable now wins over mDNS (0.4.2), so a spoofed answer no longer takes over
  while the camera is plugged in. mDNS remains the fallback when nothing is on the cable.
- Brain: unchanged tonight — it needs a design decision (§4 D-9), e.g. the brain proves it knows the token before the
  Tab5 uses it, or the MacBook's address is pinned.

> **Correction, 11 Nov 16:10 (§7.1 cycle 25).** "The screens stay built" explains only part of these numbers. The
> larger part is the glyph cache of each font size an app uses: about 0.2 kB of internal RAM per cached character,
> kept until a restart, up to 512 characters per size. Latin text fills a cache to about 100 entries and stops, which
> is why the second visit cost nothing — Chinese text keeps adding. Build c22 sizes the caches per font; with it
> 172 kB are free after opening all twelve apps.

How to read `status4`, and a wrong turn of mine:
- `int_min` showed 58–62 kB after the full walk, and at every step it sat exactly 74 kB below the free value. I first
  read that as "something takes 74 kB for a moment whenever an app opens". **Wrong.** A probe that did one action at a
  time (`~/t5/exp-c17-lowprobe.log`) showed the same 74 kB gap right after boot, before any key, and 817 samples at 30 ms
  spacing on the home screen were flat. ESP-IDF adds up one low-water mark per memory region, so the figure is a
  worst-case bound ("may be less than the real all-time minimum", IDF heap documentation), not a moment that happened.
  The gap comes from start-up, when one region was used and released.
- The figure that is a real measurement is `int_largest`, the largest free piece: 164 kB after boot, **48–62 kB** after
  all apps were opened. A single internal allocation larger than that would fail then.

What is still open:
- Not measured: free internal RAM and largest piece during a real translation turn (recording + upload + playback)
  with all apps opened.
- The remaining 161 kB are screens that stay built. Freeing them like the phrase list is possible app by app; a wider
  option is to give LVGL an allocator that prefers PSRAM (§4 D-10).

### 2.10 Software restart = boot loop (cycle 17) — fixed, cause of the regression open

- **Symptom** (03:48, build c16): console `reboot` → `rst:0xc (SW_CPU_RESET)` → display and USB come up, the SDIO link
  to the Wi-Fi chip reports "Card init success" but the chip's INIT event never arrives, the USB host sees no device,
  and after 17.7 s esp-hosted prints "Host is resetting itself" and restarts the host — the same CPU-only reset again.
  More than ten rounds in four minutes; only a hardware reset (esptool) ended it. Log: `~/t5/exp-reboot-loop.log`.
- **Why it is serious**: `esp_restart()` is also what the UI watchdog (cycle 5) and esp-hosted itself call, and a crash
  restarts the same CPU-only way. On builds c11 … c16 any of them could leave the Tab5 looping until someone resets it.
- **Intermittent, trigger unknown.** At 02:29 the same kind of restart recovered (cycle 5). After the fix I tried to
  bring the loop back on a test build (c17 plus a console command that does the CPU-only reset with the boot check
  switched off; patch in `~/t5/exp-c18t-restart-test.patch`, never committed, c17 flashed back afterwards):

  | Try | Before the CPU-only reset | Result |
  |---|---|---|
  | E1 | fresh boot, 34 s idle | recovered |
  | A | camera app opened, 105 s up | recovered |
  | B | all twelve apps opened, 129 s up | recovered |
  | C | two resets, the second 7.3 s into the boot (as at 03:48, where my command line sent `reboot` twice) | both recovered |
  | D | as C, but through `esp_restart()` with esp-hosted's own shutdown handlers — the exact 03:48 path | both recovered |

  Seven resets, seven normal boots — which looked like "not reproducible". It was only too few tries.
- **Soak (cycle 18, 04:38 → 04:53)**: the same test build, one CPU-only reset on every boot, 7.3 s after start (before
  any write to the SD card). 77 boots in 15 minutes (`~/t5/exp-c18t-soak.log`, summary `exp-c18t-soak-summary.txt`):

  | | Count |
  |---|---|
  | CPU-only resets (`rst:0xc`) | 57 |
  | … followed by a normal boot | 37 |
  | … followed by the 03:48 failure (no INIT event from the C6, esp-hosted "Restarting host") | **20 (35 %)** |
  | failures after which the next boot was `rst:0x9` and normal | **20 of 20** |
  | failing boots in which the USB host still saw the camera | 1 of 20 |

  So the fault is common, not rare: roughly one CPU-only reset in three. Two failures never came in a row here,
  because every failed boot now ends in a whole-chip reset; at 03:48, without that, the failed state held for ten rounds.
- **A lead on the cause, not a finding.** The two things that die are the two controllers that move data with DMA
  (USB host and SD/SDIO). Up to the first SDIO transfer the boot log of a failing boot is line for line the log of a
  good one, minus the USB device lines. The camera answered pings from cachy during 4 of 18 failing boots, so the
  USB-A port had power while the Tab5's USB host saw nothing: the power switches are fine, the P4 side is not. My guess
  is that a CPU-only reset in the middle of a transfer leaves those controllers stuck, and only a deeper reset frees
  them. Not tested. An earlier guess of mine (the charger pin must be driven at boot) is withdrawn: E1 … D and 37 soak
  boots recovered without it.
- **What that means for the fix**: it does not depend on the cause. A boot that follows a CPU-only reset is never
  allowed to continue, and esp-hosted's own restart goes through the whole-chip reset — shown 20 times in the failed
  state. Not tested: a real panic (same reset path as `reboot cpu` by the IDF source, not provoked).
- **Fix** (`main/restart.c`): `papp_full_reset()` arms the LP watchdog to reset the whole chip (boot log then shows
  `rst:0x9 HP_SYS_LP_WDT_RESET`, the same depth as esptool's reset, which always worked). It is registered as shutdown
  handler, so every `esp_restart()` in the firmware — ours, esp-hosted's, the C6 updater's — ends there. A crash does
  not call shutdown handlers, so `papp_restart_guard()` runs first thing in `app_main`: if the ROM reports a CPU-only
  reset it counts it (NVS `cpurst`) and does the full reset at once. NVS `rchain` stops it from doing that twice in a row.
- **Verified** on c17: T1 `reboot` — one pass, Wi-Fi connected after 12.5 s (`exp-c17-T1-reboot.log`); T2 `reboot cpu`
  — caught 1.85 s into the boot, then a normal boot, `status4 … boot_reason=0x9 cpu_resets_fixed=1`
  (`exp-c17-T2-rebootcpu.log`). Not tested: a real panic (same reset path as T2 by the IDF source, not provoked).

## 3 · Research (sources in the agents' reports, summarised)

### 3.1 ESP-NOW on the Tab5

- **Not supported by Espressif on the Tab5's own radio.** esp-hosted's coverage manifest: "ESP-NOW is not implemented in
  hosted"; issue espressif/esp-hosted-mcu#19 open since 2024. `esp_wifi_remote` ships the header, no implementation.
- A third-party route exists: ESPHome's ESP-NOW-over-CustomRpc overlay, verified by them on a Tab5. It needs a different C6
  firmware plus a host shim; a failed C6 flash is recovered only through the wired C6 header. After the trip, if at all.
- ESP-NOW between ESP32-S3 boards is the fully supported case. Facts used: 250 B payload (v1) / 1470 B (v2, IDF ≥ 5.4);
  20 peers; broadcast is never encrypted (so the HMAC stays); a device associated to an AP cannot leave that AP's channel;
  the MAC-level "success" is not an application ack (so the protocol's own acks stay).
- Other options looked at: Tab5 as SoftAP (supported, no C6 change, moderate risk), BLE (open P4+C6 bug reports), Wi-Fi
  Aware and Thread (dead ends for S3 satellites).

### 3.2 RainMaker

- "The new RainMaker" is **ESP RainMaker Neo** (announced 2026-08-05): the whole stack open source, AWS-serverless backend,
  its own CLI `esp-morpheus`. The older product is now "Classic" and still maintained.
- For this project Classic is the usable one today: Neo firmware has no ESP32-P4 target; Classic runs on P4 + hosted C6
  and Espressif's Classic camera example has an M5Stack Tab5 overlay.
- Neither SDK has ESP-NOW glue; the pattern is an S3 hub that is the RainMaker node and talks ESP-NOW to the rest.
- Needs Erick: account signup / login (never `login --password` — that puts the password in argv), phone app, claiming.
- Any RainMaker or cloud OTA on the Tab5 or the satellites means new partition tables and a USB reflash: after the trip.
- Ranked ideas (effort is an estimate): 1. CLI + account + MCP server on cachy (1–2 h); 2. a sidecar S3 node that mirrors
  line-kit KPIs and Tab5 status to the phone app (4–8 h); 3. time series and alerts on it (+2–3 h); 4. share the node with a
  client for a demo (0.5 h); 5. cloud OTA for satellites (after the trip); 6. RainMaker on the Tab5 itself (10–16 h, after
  the trip). China: Classic has a separate China cloud with separate accounts; reachability of the global cloud from
  mainland networks was not confirmed.

### 3.3 Tab5 USB-A hardware

USB-A VBUS = U27 MT9700 switch, enable = expander 0x44 pin 3, active high, about 1.3 A limit; fed from the USB-C input
(through a 1 A ideal diode) or from the battery's buck converter, so the port is powered in both cases once enabled.
USB-A is the P4's high-speed host port; USB-C is the serial/JTAG port, so console and host run together.
An ESP32-S3's built-in USB serial is a standard CDC-ACM device for the host — that is what cycle 4 uses.

### 7.2 What was wrong, and what changed

Two faults, one behind the other:

1. **Level.** "Speech = louder than 400" was tuned on the demo clips (RMS 1300–2500, studio loud). The Tab5's microphone
   at the 30 dB all modes used delivers a tenth of that. Push-to-talk, notes and the conversation mode never noticed:
   they send whole recordings and the recogniser copes with quiet audio. Only the subtitle route has a gate.
2. **Noise floor.** The floor was a running average fed by every frame under the threshold (5 % per frame). Any sound
   that is uneven but under 3 × the floor — quiet speech, a television — raised it until speech counted as noise too.
   That is why a ten times louder signal (cycle 21) still gave nothing.

| Where | Change |
|---|---|
| Tab5 `audio.c` | stream mode: 37.5 dB analog gain; noise floor = quietest 20 ms frame of the last three chunks; chunk gain = 150 / floor (1…12), moved 30 % per chunk, cut back for a chunk that would clip; level bar on a dB scale |
